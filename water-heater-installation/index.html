A planned water heater installation gives you something an emergency swap never does, which is time to choose. You can pick the fuel, put the unit where it serves the house best and fix the venting and drain details that older installs often skip. This page covers new builds, remodels, relocations and deliberate upgrades in Indian Trail, NC. If your tank is leaking right now, skip this page and read the water heater replacement page instead. That page is written for the same-day situation.

Start with the room, not the product

A water heater needs four things from its location. It needs a drain where a leak can go, enough clearance to service it, a route for venting or an electrical circuit, and protection from freezing. Garages in Union County get cold during hard freezes, so insulation on the supply lines and a thought for where the unit sits matter more than people expect.

We walk the space with you and look at the existing shutoff, the gas line size, the nearest floor drain, the vent path and the panel capacity if you are leaning electric. A good installation is mostly good planning, and the planning only takes a short visit.

What a code-minded install includes

  • A drain pan with a drain line piped to a safe termination where the space calls for it
  • A temperature and pressure relief valve with a discharge pipe that runs downward
  • A thermal expansion tank where the plumbing is a closed system
  • Shutoff valves on the water and fuel supplies
  • Dielectric or flexible connectors that suit the pipe materials
  • Proper venting for gas units, with the right material and slope

Whether a thermal expansion tank is required depends on the water supply setup and the local requirements, so we confirm it on site and do not assume it. The plumbing code in effect and the county's inspection rules decide what the job needs, and we ask the county when it is unclear.

Permits for a new or changed install

Union County Building Code Enforcement says a permit is needed for the addition or change in design of plumbing, and that a water heater swap is exempt only when there is no change in fuel, energy source, location, capacity or the routing of venting and piping. A new installation in a new location is a change in design. Plan on a permit and an inspection for a new or relocated unit, and let us handle the paperwork steps with you.

Safety items that matter on any install

A water heater has three safety features. The temperature and pressure relief valve opens if the tank gets too hot or the pressure climbs too high. The discharge pipe routes that water safely. The vent carries combustion gases outside on a gas unit. Skimping on any of them creates a hazard.

Set the thermostat at a level that is hot enough for your household and not so hot that it scalds. Many homes use a setting near 120 degrees Fahrenheit. We show you how to read and adjust the setting before we leave.

Registering and maintaining the new unit

Keep the manual, the warranty and the permit paperwork together. Register the unit with the manufacturer if the warranty requires it. Schedule a flush and an anode rod check for the future. A tank that is looked after lasts longer than one that is forgotten.

Questions about installing a water heater

How long does a planned water heater installation take?

A like-for-like gas or electric tank swap often finishes in one visit. A fuel change, a new vent run or a relocation usually takes longer, and we give you the time estimate with the quote.

Can you install in a garage or an attic?

Yes, with the right pan, drain route, access and, for gas units, the manufacturer's clearance and ignition rules. We check each space before we recommend a unit.

Do you handle the permit?

We tell you whether the county will require one and help you move through the process. Union County Building Code Enforcement has the final say.

What about a heat pump water heater?

It can work well in a space with enough room and air flow, such as a large garage or utility room. Cold closets and tight spaces are poor fits.

Can you install a unit I bought myself?

We can discuss it. The unit must suit the fuel, vent and space, and warranty terms may change when the installer did not supply the equipment.
