Tankless Water Heater Cost and Install in Indian Trail, NC

The price of the unit is only part of the bill. The gas line, vent and permit decide the rest.

Written by the Indian Trail 24/7 Plumbing team

The cost of a tankless water heater and its installation depends on the unit, the fuel, the gas line size, the vent route and the permit. A straight swap of an existing tankless is simple. Converting from a tank usually is not, because the new unit needs more gas, different venting and often new wall space. The unit price is only one part of the total.

This guide explains what drives the number, so you can judge a quote. For help with a price on your home, our tankless water heater page and our phone line are the next step.

The five items that set the price

Unit capacity and brandCapacity is rated in flow rate at a temperature rise. A bigger household or a colder incoming water needs a larger unit.
Gas lineGas tankless units use a lot of fuel at once. The existing line from the meter often needs upsizing.
VentingMost use sealed stainless or polypropylene pipe through a wall or roof, and the route and length matter.
Electrical and controlsA small circuit powers the board and fan, and electric units can need a heavy new circuit.
Permit and inspectionA change in venting and piping triggers a county permit and inspection.

Tank versus tankless: what you actually gain

A tankless unit heats water as it flows, so it does not run out the way a tank can. It takes less floor space and may last longer with care. It also costs more up front and needs annual flushing. The right choice depends on usage, space and budget.

Local note: permits and water in Indian Trail

What Union County says about water heater work

Union County Building Code Enforcement's residential page says replacing a water heater is exempt from a permit only when there is no change in fuel, energy source, location or capacity, and no change to the routing of venting and piping. It also says a permit is needed for the addition or change in design of plumbing.

Switching from a tank to a tankless unit typically changes the venting and piping, so plan for a permit and inspection. Union County Water reported very soft water at 23 ppm hardness in its 2023 report, so scale buildup in the heat exchanger is a slower problem here, but an annual flush is still the right habit.

If your home is in the county seat, the same permit rules apply county-wide. See our Monroe plumber page and the service areas page for how we cover the area.

How to compare tankless quotes

  • Ask what gas line size is being installed and why
  • Ask what vent material and route is planned
  • Ask who pulls the permit and who schedules the inspection
  • Ask if service valves for flushing are included
  • Ask whether old tank removal and haul-away are included
  • Ask about condensate drain and neutralizer on condensing units

A quote that does not mention the gas line and venting is not a full quote. For a failed tank that needs replacement today, read our water heater replacement page, because emergencies rarely allow for a tankless upgrade.

Is tankless worth it?

It can be, if your household uses lots of hot water, you lack space or you are replacing a failing tank and want a long-term unit. It can disappoint when the gas line and vent costs eat the savings or when the home has low demand. An honest plumber will tell you which side you are on.

Questions that save you money before you buy

Most expensive surprises in a tankless project come from questions nobody asked. Does the gas meter supply enough fuel for the new unit and the appliances you already run, such as a furnace and a range? Where will the vent terminate, and is that spot far enough from windows and air intakes? Does the wall you picked have the room and the support for the unit and its service valves?

If any answer is unclear, the quote is a guess. A plumber who has walked your house can tell you whether the line is large enough, whether a vent run through the garage wall is realistic and whether the electrical outlet is where it needs to be. That walk-through is the most valuable hour of the whole project.

Where tankless savings come from

A tank keeps a full volume of water hot around the clock, which wastes some energy as it cools. A tankless unit fires only when a tap opens, so it avoids that standby loss. Savings depend on how much hot water you use and what you pay for gas or electricity.

Households with low daily use see smaller savings, and the extra install cost can take a long time to earn back. Households with heavy use, frequent guests or a large soaking tub gain more, mostly in convenience, since the supply does not run out. Be wary of any quote that promises a specific payback without asking about your usage.

Maintenance you commit to

A tankless unit is not maintenance free. The heat exchanger needs a periodic flush, the inlet filter needs cleaning and the vent should be checked. Service valves, which let a plumber flush the unit in place, are cheap to add at install and awkward to add later. Make sure they are on your quote. Our tankless service page explains the flush in detail.

Tankless cost questions

Why is a tankless install so much more than a tank swap?

The unit costs more, and the job usually adds a larger gas line, new vent pipe, a condensate drain and sometimes electrical work.

Does Union County require a permit for a tankless install?

A switch from a tank to tankless usually changes venting and piping, which falls outside the permit exemption the county lists. Confirm with Union County Building Code Enforcement.

Will tankless save me money on bills?

It can use less energy because it does not keep water hot all day. Real savings depend on your usage and fuel prices.

Does soft water in Indian Trail help a tankless unit?

Yes, scale builds more slowly in very soft water. Flushing is still part of good care.

Can I install tankless in a garage?

Often yes, with correct venting, freeze protection and clearances. Check the unit's manual and local rules.
