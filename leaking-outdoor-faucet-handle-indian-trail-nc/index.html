Leaking Outdoor Faucet Handle Repair in Indian Trail, NC

Water at the handle is usually a worn packing, and it takes a wrench and a few minutes.

Hose bib repairFreeze protectionHomeowner guide

Written by the Indian Trail 24/7 Plumbing team

A leaking outdoor faucet handle is usually a worn packing washer or a loose packing nut around the stem. Tightening the nut a quarter turn often stops it. If it does not, replacing the packing or the whole stem takes a few tools and a few minutes, and the water is off for the repair.

Water that appears inside the house, in a crawlspace or in a wall near the spigot is a different problem. It can mean a frozen and split pipe, and our burst pipe repair page covers what to do.

Find where the water leaks

Leak around the handle

The packing nut or packing washer is worn. This is the most common outdoor leak.

Drip from the spout

The washer at the end of the stem no longer seals the seat.

Leak at the base on the wall

The faucet body or the connection to the pipe has failed.

Water inside the house

The pipe behind the faucet has split, often after a freeze.

Hose connection leak

A bad hose washer or a damaged thread.

Fixing a handle leak

1

Turn off the water

Close the indoor shutoff for the line if there is one, and open the faucet to drain it.

2

Tighten the packing nut

Use a wrench and turn it one quarter turn at a time, then test.

3

Replace the packing

If tightening fails, remove the handle, unscrew the nut and replace the packing washer or string packing.

4

Replace the stem if needed

A worn or pitted stem leaks past new packing. Replace it with the matching part.

5

Test under pressure

Turn the water on slowly and check the handle and the spout.

Use parts that match the make of your hose bib. A hardware store worker can match the stem from the old one. If the faucet is corroded or the body leaks, replace the whole hose bib.

Protect it from freezes

A frost-free sillcock has a long stem and a seat deep inside the wall, so the water shuts off inside the heated space. An old-style hose bib has the seat at the outside wall and holds water that freezes. Always disconnect hoses before a freeze, because a hose left attached traps water in the faucet.

TypeWhere it shuts offFreeze risk
Frost-free sillcockInside the heated spaceLow, if the hose is removed
Standard hose bibAt the outside wallHigh in a hard freeze

Local note: freezes and the Piedmont

Why a hose left on can crack a pipe here

Union County sits in the North Carolina Piedmont, where winter weather swings between mild days and hard overnight freezes. A faucet that survives most winters can split in the one cold snap when a hose stays connected. The split often shows up only after the thaw, when water leaks through the wall.

Many homes in Indian Trail were built after 1990 and carry hose bibs on exterior garage and rear walls. Those spots are exposed. Take a minute on a freezing night to disconnect hoses and open the indoor valve if you have one. Ask Union County Water or your utility for its guidance on meter box and shutoff locations if you are unsure where to find them.

Our Monroe plumber page explains how we cover the county seat, and the service areas page lists other nearby communities.

When to call a plumber

Call if the leak is inside the wall, if the faucet is corroded and will not turn, if tightening does not stop the leak or if you find water in the crawlspace. Those jobs involve cutting into the pipe, which is a plumbing repair.

Tools and parts for the repair

You do not need much. An adjustable wrench or a basin wrench, a screwdriver, a replacement packing washer or graphite packing string and, if needed, a new stem that matches the faucet are enough for most handle leaks. Take the old washer or stem to the store so the match is right.

  • An adjustable wrench that fits the packing nut
  • A screwdriver for the handle screw
  • Replacement packing washers or string packing
  • A matching repair stem for the make of your faucet
  • Thread seal tape for any threaded fittings you disturb
  • A bucket and a rag for the water that drains from the line

Signs the problem is bigger than the handle

A faucet that drips from the spout after you close it, a handle that spins without shutting the water off and a stem that has pitted from corrosion usually need a new stem or a whole new hose bib. Water inside the house, a damp wall or a wet crawlspace near the faucet means the pipe behind it is leaking.

Do not keep tightening a packing nut that is already hard to turn. Over-tightening can crack the fitting or lock the handle. If the leak does not stop after a modest turn and a fresh washer, the stem is the likelier cause.

Winter habits that save the faucet

Disconnect the hose at the first forecast freeze and leave it off until spring. Close the indoor shutoff to the line if there is one, open the outside faucet to drain it and leave it open. A foam cover over the faucet helps a little but does not replace draining the line.

A simple routine, done once in the fall, prevents the most expensive outdoor plumbing repair most homeowners ever face. If you are not sure where the indoor shutoff is, walk the wall behind the faucet and look in the garage or the crawlspace.

Outdoor faucet questions

Why does my outdoor faucet leak when it is turned on?

A worn packing nut or packing washer lets water seep around the stem when the faucet is open.

Can I tighten the packing nut myself?

Yes. A quarter turn at a time with a wrench is safe. Stop if the handle stiffens.

Will a frost-free sillcock still freeze?

It can if a hose stays connected, because water trapped in the faucet cannot drain.

Is a leak inside the wall after a freeze urgent?

Yes. Shut off the water to that line and call the 24/7 line. Water in a wall causes damage fast.

Does Union County require a permit to replace a hose bib?

A like-for-like replacement is generally minor work. Confirm with Union County Building Code Enforcement if you change the pipe.
