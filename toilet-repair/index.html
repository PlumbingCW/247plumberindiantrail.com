A toilet that will not stop running or keeps rising toward the rim is a problem you want solved fast. Our line is open 24/7, and the plumber on the phone can walk you through stopping an overflow before anyone arrives. In most cases you can stop the water yourself in about ten seconds.

Lift the tank lid and push the flapper down, or reach behind the toilet and turn the small oval valve clockwise until it stops. That shuts the supply to this toilet only. Then stop flushing and call us.

When a toilet problem is urgent

A toilet that overflows with clean tank water is a mess. One that overflows with waste is a health issue. If several fixtures are backing up, the blockage is probably in the main line, and our sewer line cleaning page explains what to do. A single toilet that is stubbornly blocked is usually a trap or short branch issue we clear with a closet auger.

A toilet that leaks at the base can rot the subfloor beneath it over months, so a steady drip at the floor is worth a call even when nothing looks dramatic.

Constant running

Usually a worn flapper, a bad fill valve or a chain that is too tight or too loose.

Weak flush

Clogged rim jets or a partial blockage in the trap or drain line.

Rocking or loose

The flange bolts or the wax ring have failed. Left alone it leaks at the floor.

Leak at the base

A failed wax ring or a cracked bowl. It needs repair before the floor is damaged.

Phantom refill

The tank refills every few minutes because the flapper leaks into the bowl.

Sweating tank

Condensation on a cold tank. It is not a leak but can be reduced.

What we check and fix

We start with the supply valve, the fill valve and the flapper, since these three cause most running toilets. A dye tablet in the tank shows whether the flapper leaks. For clogs, a closet auger clears the trap without scratching the bowl. When the toilet rocks or leaks at the floor, we pull it, replace the wax ring and check the flange and floor for damage.

If your toilet is old and needs repeated work, we compare repair cost with replacement. Newer models use far less water per flush, which affects your bill. The faucet repair and plumbing installation pages cover related fixture work.

A note on water use in Union County

A running toilet can waste a lot of water in a day, and the bill shows it. If your meter keeps moving with everything off, a toilet is the first fixture to test. Union County Water reports soft water and near-neutral pH, so mineral buildup in tanks is less common here than in hard-water areas, and failures are mostly wear on rubber parts and seals.

Many Indian Trail homes were built since 1990 and carry their original toilets and fill valves. Rubber parts age in a way that does not look like much until the toilet starts running at 2 a.m.

Choosing between repair and a new toilet

A toilet that needs a flapper or a fill valve is almost always worth fixing. A toilet that cracks, clogs often or wastes water may be better replaced. A new high-efficiency toilet can flush well with less water, and many models are taller and more comfortable. We install what you choose and haul away the old toilet.

If your toilet is a model that is decades old, parts may be hard to find. Replacement parts for obscure models cost more and take longer. Replacing the toilet in that case is practical.

After a toilet repair

Flush a few times and watch the base for leaks. Check that the toilet is stable and that the seat is secure. Make sure the water shuts off at the right level and the tank does not run. A quick test by the plumber and by you before the visit ends prevents a callback.

Leaking and running toilets

A running toilet and a leaking toilet are different problems. A running toilet moves water from the tank into the bowl or overflow tube through a worn flapper, a stuck fill valve or a misadjusted float. A leaking toilet drips water on the floor from the tank bolts, the supply line or the base. Wet floor around the toilet is urgent, because water can soak into the subfloor.

Dry the area, then watch to see where the water comes from. Tighten the supply line nut by hand, check the tank bolts and look at the wax ring seal at the base. If the leak is at the base, the toilet needs to be reset with a new wax ring. Close the valve behind the toilet to stop the water while you wait.

Toilet repair questions

Do you handle toilet repair emergencies at night or on weekends in Indian Trail?

Yes. The 24/7 line is open every day. Close the valve behind the toilet first, then call.

Why does my toilet run on its own?

A leaking flapper or a stuck fill valve is the usual reason. Both are quick repairs.

Is it okay to use chemical drain cleaner in a toilet?

We advise against it. It can damage parts and is dangerous if it splashes during plunging or snaking.

Will a toilet repair take long?

Most repairs finish in one visit, often in under an hour for common parts.

Do you serve Monroe?

Yes. See our Monroe page.

When should I replace instead of repair?

If the toilet is cracked, constantly clogs or uses far more water than newer models, replacement can pay off.
