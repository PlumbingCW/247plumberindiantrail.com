A disposal that stops working turns a kitchen into a problem. The usual complaints are a hum with no spinning, a leak under the sink or a sink that fills with gray water. Our line is open 24/7, and when water is leaking under the sink, the plumber can tell you how to shut it off first.

Never put your hand into a disposal, even when it is off. Turn off the wall switch and the breaker if the unit is hardwired before any inspection, and use tongs or pliers to remove an object.

Disposal emergency: what the symptoms mean

Hum without spinning

The blades are jammed. A hex key turned in the bottom socket often frees it, then reset the red button.

No sound at all

A tripped reset button, a flipped breaker or a failed motor.

Leak from the bottom

A cracked housing or failed seal, usually meaning replacement.

Leak at the top or sides

A loose flange, a failed gasket or a drain connection.

Sink backs up

The clog is past the disposal in the drain line, not in the unit.

Bad smell

Food buildup in the chamber or the drain. A cleaning often solves it.

Most jams are small items or bones. Citrus peel, coffee grounds and fibrous vegetables can pack into a clog in the line. If the problem is the drain and not the disposal, see our drain cleaning page.

Repair or replace

A jam or a loose connection is a repair. A leak from the housing is not, because the shell is the pressure boundary. An old unit with a failing motor is usually cheaper to replace than to rebuild. We replace with a model sized for your household and your sink, and we connect the dishwasher drain and the power correctly.

Many disposals last around eight to twelve years. Replacement also lets you upgrade the mounting and the sound insulation. A continuous-feed model suits most homes.

Kitchen drains in Indian Trail homes

Many homes here are 1990s and later builds with PVC drains and a standard double sink. Grease is the main long-term enemy of any kitchen line, and a disposal grinds food but does not remove grease from the pipe wall. If sinks clog even after the disposal is fixed, a snake or jetting visit clears the line.

Union County Water reports soft water. That has no effect on a disposal, but the chloramine in the water supply and normal kitchen use mean rubber seals still wear. We replace gaskets and splash guards as part of a good repair.

Safe ways to free a jam

Turn off the wall switch and unplug the unit, or turn off its breaker. Look into the chamber with a flashlight and remove any object with tongs. Insert a hex key into the socket at the bottom of the unit and work it back and forth to free the blades. Press the red reset button on the bottom and restore power. Do not reach into the chamber with your hand.

If the unit still hums, the motor may be seized. If it does nothing, check the breaker and the reset again. A disposal that trips repeatedly is telling you the motor is failing, and replacement is usually wiser than repeated resets.

Habits that extend disposal life

Run plenty of cold water while grinding. Cut large scraps into smaller pieces. Skip grease, eggshells in bulk, fibrous items like celery strings and starchy foods that turn to paste. A clean disposal smells better and wears more slowly. Periodically grind citrus peel or a handful of ice to scrub the chamber, and clean the rubber splash guard by hand.

Disposal questions

Do you handle garbage disposal repair emergencies at night or on weekends in Indian Trail?

Yes. Turn off the unit and close the water supply to the sink if you can, and call the 24/7 line.

Can I hire a handyman to install a garbage disposal?

A licensed plumber is best when the drain, the dishwasher connection and the power all need to be correct. A poor install can leak into the cabinet.

What should never go in a disposal?

Grease, fibrous stalks, coffee grounds in bulk, pasta and rice in large amounts, and non-food items.

How do I clean the smell?

Run cold water and grind ice and citrus peel in small amounts, or have us clean the chamber and line.
