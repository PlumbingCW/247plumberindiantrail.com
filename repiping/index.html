Repiping means replacing the supply lines throughout a house. It is a big decision, so the first job is deciding whether you need it at all. If pipes are leaking repeatedly, water is discolored or pressure has fallen, we can assess the system and tell you plainly whether repairs or a repipe is the better money.

This is a planned job, not usually an emergency. If a pipe has failed right now, our line is open 24/7. Shut off the main and call, then read about burst pipe repair.

Signs you may need a repipe

Repeated pinhole leaks

One leak is a repair. Several in a year signal the system is wearing out.

Discolored or metallic water

Rust brown or orange water from taps can mean corroding galvanized pipe.

Low pressure throughout

Internal buildup narrows old pipe and chokes flow.

Known problem pipe

Polybutylene and some early fittings have a poor record and are often replaced.

Major remodel

Opening walls anyway is a good time to replace old supply lines.

Pipe materials and what they mean

Homes built in the 1990s growth period can have copper, CPVC or, in some cases, polybutylene, which was widely used until the mid-1990s and has a record of failure. Galvanized steel in older houses corrodes from the inside. If you do not know what you have, we identify it by looking at exposed sections.

PEX is the common replacement today. It is flexible, resists freezing damage better than rigid pipe and needs fewer joints. Copper remains a quality option, and the right choice depends on your home and budget.

How a repipe works

1

Assess the system

We map the existing lines, pressure and fixtures and decide on whole house or partial.

2

Plan the route

We choose a path with the least wall and ceiling opening.

3

Install and connect

New lines run to each fixture from a manifold or trunk, then connect to the water heater.

4

Test and patch

We pressure test, restore water and leave access openings ready for drywall repair.

Local factors in Indian Trail

Union County Water reported soft water at 23 ppm hardness and a pH of 7.11 in its 2023 report. That mild chemistry means corrosion from the water itself is limited, so failures tend to trace to fittings, pipe material defects and installation issues. We look at the cause before recommending a repipe, because a single bad run does not always justify the whole house.

Union County Building Code Enforcement states that a permit is needed for the addition or change in design of plumbing. A repipe usually involves a permit and an inspection, so we handle that with you and confirm requirements for your address.

Living through a repipe

A repipe is disruptive for a few days. Walls and ceilings are opened in places, water is off for part of each day and workers move around the house. Good crews protect floors, keep the work area tidy and tell you when water will be off. You can help by clearing access to walls, moving valuables and planning for showers elsewhere on the day the main lines are swapped.

Ask how the crew handles drywall. Some leave the openings for a separate trade, and some patch to a paintable state. Knowing that up front keeps your expectations and your schedule realistic.

After the work

Once the new lines are in, we pressure test them and check each fixture. You should see steady pressure and hot water that arrives without delay at the farthest tap. Run each faucet for a minute to clear debris and replace the aerators if they clog. Keep the permit documents and the manufacturer's information with your house records. They matter when you sell the house.

Codes and safety steps for a repipe

A repipe is permitted work under the State Plumbing Code, which adopts the International Plumbing Code (IPC), and inspections happen before walls are closed. Plan for a safe work area. Keep children and pets out of open walls, and ask the crew where the water will be off.

  • Know where your main shutoff is before the crew arrives
  • Clear access to walls, closets and the water heater
  • Do not use the water heater while lines are open
  • Keep the permit paperwork with your house records

Repiping questions

Do you handle urgent pipe failures at night in Indian Trail?

Yes. For a burst or leaking line, call the 24/7 line. A repipe itself is scheduled work.

How long does a repipe take?

Many houses take a few days, depending on size and access. We give a time estimate with the quote.

Will I have water during the repipe?

There will be a gap while lines are swapped, and we plan it to reduce disruption.

Is my polybutylene pipe a problem?

It has a record of failure, so many owners replace it. We can identify it and advise.

Do you repipe homes in Monroe?

Yes. See the Monroe page.

Can I repipe just part of the house?

Yes. A partial repipe addresses the worst section when the rest of the system is sound.
