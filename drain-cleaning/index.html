When a clogged drain turns into an emergency

One slow sink is an inconvenience. Several fixtures backing up together is not. If flushing the toilet makes the tub gurgle or burble, or water from the washer appears in a basement floor drain, the blockage is probably in the main line, and every gallon you add has nowhere to go.

Raw sewage on a floor is also a health problem. Wear boots and gloves if you must walk through it, keep it away from food and bedding and call right away. Our sewer line cleaning page covers what happens when the clog sits past the house wall.

One fixture slow

Usually hair, soap film or food in the trap or the branch line. A snake or a trap cleaning often fixes it in one visit.

Two or more fixtures slow

The shared branch or the main line is narrowing. This needs a longer cable and often a camera look.

Gurgling after a flush

Air is being pushed past a partial blockage. Treat it as an early warning of a main line clog.

Water in the lowest drain

A basement or garage floor drain is the lowest opening in the system, so it shows a main line backup first.

Foul smell with no clog

A dry trap, a cracked vent or a slow buildup in the line. We smoke-test or camera the line to find out.

How we clear and confirm

We start with the least invasive tool that will work. A hand auger handles most sink and tub clogs. A motorized cable machine with the right cutting head reaches kitchen branch lines and main lines. When grease, scale or roots have coated the pipe wall, a cable only punches a hole, and the clog returns, so we talk to you about hydro jetting instead.

We confirm the fix by running water, watching the flow and checking that nearby fixtures drain normally. If the line looks damaged or keeps clogging in the same spot, we recommend a sewer camera inspection so you can see the pipe instead of guessing.

What clogs drains in newer Union County homes

Indian Trail grew from 1,942 people in 1990 to 39,997 in the 2020 census, so most drain lines here are modern PVC, not old cast iron. That is good news for roots and corrosion, and less good news for the common culprits in newer homes. Flushable wipes, kitchen grease, long hair and builder debris left in a new line are the usual causes.

Many subdivisions built in the 2000s were landscaped with young trees close to the lateral. Those trees are now mature, and roots find the smallest joint gap. If your clog returns every few months in the same fixture, ask for a camera look before paying for another snake.

Codes and sources for drain work

Drain, trap and vent rules in North Carolina come from the State Plumbing Code, which adopts the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with amendments. That code is why every fixture needs a trap and a vent, and it is the reason a gurgling drain is worth a look. Local utility rules decide who handles the public main, so check with your utility if a backup affects neighbors too.

Kitchen sink clogs

Kitchen drains clog differently from bathroom drains. Grease and food waste cool in the pipe, harden and build a ring that narrows the line until a slow drain stops entirely. Running hot water or dish soap clears the film for a while, but the buildup keeps growing. A dishwasher that drains into the same line makes it worse, and a garbage disposal moves food into the pipe without removing grease.

For a kitchen clog we use a cable with a cutting head sized for the line, and for heavy grease we recommend jetting. Do not pour grease down the drain, scrape plates into the trash and run hot water for a few seconds after using the disposal. If the sink and the dishwasher both back up, the clog is past the shared trap.

Snaking and cabling: what the work involves

A cable, sometimes called a snake, is a coiled steel line that spins through the pipe. A small hand auger handles fixtures. A motorized machine pushes a longer cable with a cutting head through branch and main lines. The tech feeds the cable until it meets the blockage, works it through, then pulls it back and flushes the line.

Cabling works well for hair, paper and soft clogs. It is less effective on hard grease or heavy roots, because the cable cuts a hole and leaves the walls coated. We tell you which result to expect and when jetting is the better tool. We also run water after the job to confirm the line drains at full speed.

Shower and tub drains, and leaking drain lines

Showers and tubs clog with hair and soap. Many can be cleared by pulling the hair out of the drain with a small tool, and a cable handles the rest. If a tub drains slowly even after cleaning, the trap or the overflow may be the issue.

A drain line can also leak. Signs include a stain under a bathroom, a musty smell or water that appears only when a fixture is draining. A leaking drain line is not a clog and a cable will not fix it. We confirm with a water test or a camera and repair the section that has failed.

Drain cleaning questions from Indian Trail homeowners

Do you handle drain cleaning emergencies at night or on weekends in Indian Trail?

Yes. The 24/7 line is answered every day. If sewage is backing up or several fixtures are affected, tell us right away and we will treat it as urgent.

Will pouring a drain cleaner from the store fix it?

Chemical cleaners can damage older fixtures and are hard on a plumber's skin and eyes if the line is still full. If a bottle has not worked on the first try, stop and call.

How do I know if the clog is in the main line?

If more than one fixture is slow, or the lowest drain in the house shows water when you run an upstairs fixture, the main line is the likely location.

Is a camera inspection worth it?

When a drain clogs repeatedly, yes. It shows roots, bellies, offsets or broken joints, and it tells you whether cleaning is enough or a repair is needed.

Do you serve Monroe as well as Indian Trail?

We do. See the Monroe plumber page for how we cover the county seat.

What can I do to keep drains clear?

Keep grease out of the sink, use a hair catcher in showers, and flush only toilet paper. Wipes labeled flushable are a leading cause of clogs.
