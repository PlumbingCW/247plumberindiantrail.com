The water service line runs from the meter to your house, usually buried a foot or more below the surface. When it breaks, you see a wet patch in the yard, a drop in pressure or an unexplained bill. A line that has failed completely sends water across a yard and can undermine a driveway. Our line is open 24/7.

If you see water bubbling up or flowing, close the valve at the house or at the meter and call us. Do not dig near the area before the lines are marked, since gas and electric may share the route.

Water line emergency: signs the service line is leaking

Wet or soft spot in the yard

Water finds the path of least resistance and surfaces along the pipe route.

Low pressure

A leak before the house reduces what reaches your fixtures.

Meter spinning with everything off

The meter counts water leaving, wherever it goes.

Sound of water at the meter

A hissing near the meter or the shutoff means water is escaping upstream.

Unexpectedly green or sunken lawn strip

Constant leakage waters one strip and softens the ground above it.

How we locate and repair it

We confirm the leak with a meter test and a pressure check, then trace the line with a locator and listening equipment. A small dig exposes the pipe at the break. We cut out the damaged section and splice in new pipe with the right fittings, or replace more of the line if it is in poor shape.

Pipe material matters. Many service lines in newer areas are polyethylene or copper, and older lines elsewhere may be galvanized. Union County Water reports a near-neutral pH of 7.11, so corrosion from water chemistry is less of a driver than damage from digging, settling and fittings.

Who owns what on the water line

The utility owns the meter and generally the line up to it. The homeowner is usually responsible for the pipe from the meter to the house. Where the boundary sits can vary, so confirm with Union County Water or your utility before you plan repairs. Calling the utility early also gets the meter shutoff handled correctly.

Always call 811 before digging. It is free and marks public utilities. Private lines may not be marked, which is why locating equipment matters.

What the repair involves

A service line repair means digging a trench, so we plan around driveways, sidewalks and landscaping. Where possible we choose a route that avoids concrete. We restore water as soon as the pipe is spliced and tested, and we backfill with care so the pipe does not settle again.

If the problem is a pressure issue inside the house, we may also check the regulator. A high-pressure supply stresses your pipes and fixtures over time, and a regulator fixes that.

What happens at the meter

The meter and the valve around it belong to the utility, and rules about who may operate them vary. We coordinate shutoffs with the utility when needed and do not tamper with equipment that is theirs. If the repair requires the water to be off at the meter, we plan that so you are without water for as short a time as possible.

If you suspect the leak is before the meter, call the utility. A leak on their side is their repair and not yours. A leak after the meter is yours, and we can fix it.

Backfill and settling

A repaired trench needs careful backfill. We place soil in layers and compact it so the ground does not settle over the new pipe. A trench that is filled too fast sinks later and can break the line again or leave a depression in the lawn. Where the trench crosses a driveway or a walk, we discuss the restoration with you before we start.

Low water pressure: line or house

Low pressure can come from the street, from the service line, from the pressure regulator or from the plumbing inside the house. Test a hose bib near the meter. If pressure is good there and weak at upstairs fixtures, the problem is inside the house. If it is weak at the hose bib too, suspect the service line, a partly closed valve or the regulator.

A leak in the buried line, a corroded section of old pipe or a stuck pressure reducing valve all cut pressure. A gauge screwed onto a hose bib shows the number in seconds. Pressure that is too high is also a problem, since it stresses fixtures and joints. We check both ends of the range.

Buried line leaks that do not surface

Not every buried leak shows up as a wet spot. In sandy or well-draining soil the water can disappear downward. The clues are an unexplained rise in the bill, a meter that moves with the house shut off and a faint hiss near the meter. We trace the line with a locator and listening equipment and mark the spot before any digging.

Service line questions

Do you handle water service line repair emergencies at night or on weekends in Indian Trail?

Yes. The 24/7 line is open every day. Shut the water off at the house valve or meter and call us.

How do I tell if the leak is before or after the meter?

Shut the house valve and watch the meter. If it still moves, the leak is between the meter and the valve. If not, it is inside the house.

Will you dig up my whole yard?

No. We locate the break and open only as much as needed to repair it.

Who pays, me or the utility?

Usually the homeowner pays for the line from the meter to the house. Confirm with your utility.

Do you serve Monroe too?

Yes. See the Monroe page.

Is a high pressure problem related?

It can be. We test pressure and can install or replace a regulator.
